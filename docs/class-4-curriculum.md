- **Exact Chapter Name**: 2. 3D PEN - II (or 2. 3D PEN)
- **Topics**:
  1. What is 3D? (width, height, depth; 2D vs 3D spatial dimensions)
  2. What is 3D Pen? (handheld free-form structure device, molten plastic filament extrusion)
  3. Parts of 3D Pen:
     - 1. Filament Input Hole
     - 2. Power Input Hole
     - 3. Power Indicator LED (Red = power on)
     - 4. Work Indicator LED (Green = heated and ready at 190°C)
     - 5. LCD Screen (temperature monitor)
     - 6. Temperature Adjustment Buttons (+ / -)
     - 7. Speed Controller (flow speed: slow neat lines vs fast filling)
     - 8. Load Filament Button (downward arrow)
     - 9. Unload Filament Button (upward arrow)
     - 10. Integrated Heating Nozzle (hot tip extrusion zone)
  4. Working & Operating Steps:
     - Step 1: Plug in and switch ON, press downward arrow to begin heating.
     - Step 2: Temperature reaches 190°C, red LED turns Green.
     - Step 3: Insert filament into input hole, press feed button.
     - Step 4: Draw 3D structures as filament extrudes.
     - Step 5: Press upward arrow to unload filament.
     - Step 6: Cool down for 5 minutes before cleaning/storage.
  5. Benefits of 3D Printing Pens for Students:
     - Enhances Creativity, Introduces STEM Concepts, Hands-on Learning, Problem-Solving Skills, Accessibility to 3D Printing, Boosts Motor Skills.

## Chapter 3: GEARS (Pages 24–42)
- **Exact Chapter Name**: 3. GEARS
- **Topics**:
  1. Introduction to Gears (transfer motion and power, equal spacing teeth on cylindrical/conical surfaces, driver to driven transmission)
  2. Examples of Objects with Gears: Clocks, mixers, automobiles, motors, bicycles
  3. Parts of a Gear: Teeth (ridges), Edge (outer rim), Hub (center mounting), Hole/Bore (shaft opening)
  4. Primary (Driver) and Secondary (Driven) Gears; speed measured in RPM (revolutions per minute)
  5. Working of Gears: Matched pairs mesh without slippage
  6. Types of Gears:
     - Spur Gears (parallel shafts, simplest and most common)
     - Bevel Gears (shafts at an angle, usually 90 degrees, conical teeth)
     - Screw Gears / Crossed Helical (non-intersecting, non-parallel shafts)
  7. Gear Ratios and Speed:
     - Definition: ratio of output turns to input turns
     - Formula: $\text{Gear Ratio} = \frac{\text{Number of teeth on driven gear}}{\text{Number of teeth on driving gear}}$
     - Impact: Higher gear ratio = slower speed, greater torque; Lower gear ratio = faster speed, less torque
     - Worked example: 100-tooth driver gear drives 25-tooth driven gear $\rightarrow \text{Ratio} = 25/100 = 1/4$ (speed multiplier)
- **Lessons**:
  - Lesson 3.1: Mechanics of Gears & 4 Anatomy Parts
  - Lesson 3.2: Primary (Driver) vs Secondary (Driven) Gears & RPM
  - Lesson 3.3: Types of Gears (Spur, Bevel at 90°, Screw/Crossed Helical)
  - Lesson 3.4: Gear Ratios, Torque vs Speed Physics
- **Fun Facts**:
  - Gears have been used for over 2,000 years! Archimedes used gears in water clocks.
- **Models**:
  - **MODEL 1 - 4-Wheel-Drive car!** (Experiment 9): All 4 wheels powered by a single motor using G20 idlers and G60 gears for rough terrain.
  - **MODEL 2 - Car with a real working gear box (R-N-1-2)** (Experiment 10): Multi-speed transmission with Reverse, Neutral, 1st gear, and 2nd gear manual shifter.
- **Activities**:
  - Activity-2: Marble Run using cardboard and cups (U-shaped track, continuous wall path).
- **Questions & Exercises**:
  - 10 MCQs (Main purpose, Teeth function, Hub connection, Primary driver gear, Meshing without slippage, Spur gear parallel shafts, Bevel gear 90° angle, Gear ratio formula, Higher gear ratio torque, Real applications).
  - Short Answer Questions:

## Chapter 5: ELECTRO MAGNETICS - I (Pages 75–86)
- **Exact Chapter Name**: 5. ELECTRO MAGNETICS-I
- **Topics**:
  1. Introduction to Electromagnetics:
     - Electro (electric field) + Magnetism (magnetic field) interacting with charged particles
     - Electricity: flow of electric charge; protons (+), electrons (-), neutrons (neutral)
     - Electric Circuit: path along which electric current flows (source, conductor, load)
     - Magnetism: attraction/repulsion force caused by motion of electric charges
     - Magnetic Field & Poles: North & South poles; like poles repel, unlike poles attract
     - Mechanical waves vs Electromagnetic waves
  2. Components Used for Electromagnetics:
     - Magnets (horseshoe magnet, bar magnets)
     - LEDs (Light Emitting Diodes: long-lasting, energy-efficient, Anode [+] longer leg, Cathode [-] shorter leg, circuit symbol)
     - Motors (electrical to mechanical motion)
     - Wires (conductive copper paths)
     - Buzzers (electrical to acoustic sound signals)
  3. Open Circuit vs Closed Circuit:
     - Open Circuit: broken path, current cannot flow
     - Closed Circuit: unbroken loop, current flows uninterrupted
     - Battery Terminals: Positive (+) Anode/red (high potential), Negative (-) Cathode/black (electron source)
  4. Conductors vs Insulators:
     - Conductors: copper, aluminum, silver, iron, sea water
     - Insulators: rubber, plastic, wood, air, oil, Styrofoam
     - Paper clip switch circuit experiment
- **Lessons**:
  - Lesson 5.1: Electricity, Charges & Magnetic Poles
  - Lesson 5.2: Anatomy of Circuit Components (LED Anode/Cathode, Motor, Buzzer)
  - Lesson 5.3: Open Circuit vs Closed Circuit Mechanics
  - Lesson 5.4: Conductors vs Insulators in Circuits
- **Fun Facts**:
  - Electricity and magnetism are best friends! Current flowing in a wire creates a magnetic field, and a moving magnet creates electricity. This is how all motors and generators work.
- **Activities**:
  - Activity-4: Word Hunt puzzle (10 words: ELECTRICITY, VOLTAGE, WIRE, CONDUCTOR, INSULATOR, CURRENT, INDICATOR, ENERGY, PARALLEL, SERIES).
- **Questions & Exercises**:

## Chapter 6: ELECTRO MAGNETICS - II (Pages 87–94)
- **Exact Chapter Name**: 6. ELECTRO MAGNETICS-II
- **Topics**:
  1. Series vs Parallel Circuits:
     - Series Circuits: connected end-to-end; single path; same current flows through all; total voltage = sum of voltage drops; if one component breaks, entire circuit stops
     - Parallel Circuits: connected across each other; multiple paths; current splits across branches; voltage is equal across all branches; devices operate independently
  2. Logic Gates (decision-making building blocks):
     - Definition: digital elements taking 0 (OFF) or 1 (ON) inputs to produce an output
     - AND Gate: output is 1 ONLY if all inputs are 1 (both switches ON)
     - OR Gate: output is 1 if AT LEAST ONE input is 1 (either switch ON)
  3. Practical Electronic Experiments:
     - Water Level Indicator: combination of overflow alarm and parallel circuit
     - Water Flow Alarm: buzzer and battery circuit with wire probes at the rim of a glass; water conducts electricity to trigger the buzzer alarm
- **Lessons**:
  - Lesson 6.1: Series vs Parallel Circuits (Current & Voltage Rules)
  - Lesson 6.2: Logic Gates (AND Gate vs OR Gate Decision Tables)
  - Lesson 6.3: Water Level Indicator & Water Flow Alarm Experiments
- **Fun Facts**:
  - Why your house isn't wired in series: if it were, you'd have to turn on the microwave, toaster, and all lights just to watch TV! Parallel wiring allows independent device control.
- **Experiments**:
  - Experiment A: Water Level Indicator (LED + Buzzer + parallel wire probes).
  - Experiment B: Water Flow Alarm (glass rim conductivity probe triggering buzzer alarm).
- **Questions & Exercises**:
  - 10 MCQs (Series circuit end-to-end, Same current in series, Voltage drop sum in series, Parallel multiple paths, Current splits in parallel, Voltage identical in parallel branches, Logic gate function, AND gate condition, OR gate condition, Water conducting alarm).
  - Short Answer Questions:

## Glossary (Pages 106–107)
Full alphabetical dictionary of 58 STEM terms:
1. **Acceleration** — Increase in speed.
2. **Actuator** — Part that causes movement in a robot.
3. **Algorithm** — Step-by-step instructions to solve a problem.
4. **Automation** — Working automatically without human control.
5. **Axle** — A rod that holds gears or wheels and helps them rotate.
6. **Battery** — A device that stores and gives electrical power.
7. **Block Coding** — Coding using ready-made blocks.
8. **Circuit** — A complete path for electricity to flow.
9. **Closed Circuit** — A complete path where electricity flows.
10. **Compound Machine** — A machine made of two or more simple machines.
11. **Condition** — A decision in coding (if/then).
12. **Conductor** — Material that allows electricity to pass through.
13. **Controller** — The brain of the robot.
14. **Current** — Flow of electricity in a circuit.
15. **Debugging** — Finding and fixing mistakes in a program.
16. **Driver Gear** — The gear that starts the movement.
17. **Driven Gear** — The gear that receives movement.
18. **Eco-friendly** — Safe for the environment.
19. **Efficiency** — How well a machine works without wasting energy.
20. **Electricity** — A type of energy that powers devices.
21. **Force** — A push or pull.
22. **Friction** — A force that slows down movement.
23. **Fulcrum** — The fixed point of a lever.
24. **Gear** — A toothed wheel that transfers motion.
25. **Gear Ratio** — The relationship between the sizes of two gears.
26. **Gear Train** — A set of connected gears.
27. **Hydraulic System** — A system that uses liquid pressure to create movement.
28. **Insulator** — A material that blocks electricity.
29. **Lever** — A bar that moves around a fixed point to lift objects.
30. **Load** — The weight carried by a machine.
31. **Loop** — Repeating instructions in coding.
32. **Mechanical Advantage** — How much a machine makes work easier.
33. **Mechanics** — The study of how machines and objects move.
34. **Motion** — Movement or change in position of an object.
35. **Motor** — A device that converts electricity into movement.
36. **Open Circuit** — A broken path where electricity cannot flow.
37. **Pollution** — Harmful substances that damage the environment.
38. **Programming** — Giving instructions to a computer or robot.
39. **Pulley** — A wheel with a rope used to lift heavy objects.
40. **Renewable Energy** — Energy from natural sources like sun and wind.
41. **Robot** — A machine that performs tasks automatically.
42. **RPM (Revolutions Per Minute)** — Number of turns in one minute.
43. **Scratch** — A block-based coding platform for beginners.
44. **Sensor** — A device that detects changes like light, sound, or distance.
45. **Simple Machine** — A basic tool that makes work easier.
46. **Solar Energy** — Energy from the sun.
47. **Solar Panel** — A device that converts sunlight into electricity.
48. **Speed** — How fast something moves.
49. **Sustainability** — Using resources carefully without wasting them.
50. **Switch** — A device used to turn electricity ON or OFF.
51. **Torque** — Turning force.
52. **Transmission** — A system that transfers power from one part to another.
53. **Ultrasonic Sensor** — A sensor that measures distance using sound waves.
54. **Variable** — A place to store information in coding.
55. **Velocity** — Speed with direction.
56. **Voltage** — Electrical pressure in a circuit.
57. **Wind Energy** — Energy from moving air.
